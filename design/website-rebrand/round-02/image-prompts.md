## Circuit tracing

Use case: stylized-concept.
Create an original landscape editorial print, 3:2 aspect ratio, for Carolina AI Alignment. This is finished artwork only, with absolutely no typography.

ART DIRECTION — THE MOST IMPORTANT REQUIREMENT:
A rigorously FLAT, TWO-INK SILKSCREEN PRINT in the tradition of 1960s Swiss concrete graphic art and experimental scientific book covers, with the optical precision of an engraved guilloche plate. Not a picture of a physical object. Not a rendered sculpture. Not a diagram made with a software UI. Think a spare, confident museum poster made from hand-pulled printing plates: strong negative space, controlled asymmetry, obsessive parallel linework, saturated pigment, tiny purposeful imperfections. The entire image must have one unequivocal graphic language. It should look designed and printed, not illustrated by a general-purpose AI.

SUBJECT / IDEA:
Mechanistic interpretability: revealing a single meaningful route inside a complicated system. Represent the system as a large oval woven field made from about sixty fine parallel Carolina-blue lines. The lines enter from the left as straight parallel tracks, bend into one broad looping knot in the center, weave over and under another fan of lines, then resolve into evenly spaced horizontal tracks exiting on the right. The knot should form a single sophisticated asymmetrical optical figure, occupying the central 75% of the frame. It has open eye-shaped apertures, and the white paper is visible between EVERY blue line. Some paths curve upward around the aperture; some downward. It should evoke a carved mathematical surface without depicting a three-dimensional physical surface.

The key conceptual gesture: ONE continuous, moderately thick vermilion-orange thread is isolated from the blue weave. It begins around x=12%, y=67%, follows the logic of the weave through its central aperture, emerges around x=73%, y=31%, and ends in a single small filled orange circle near x=86%, y=33%. Its route should be immediately legible. The colored thread must actually weave through the structure, with a few precise interruptions for over/under crossings; it is not a random line draped on top. One small orange circle at the route end, no other circles or nodes. Keep the orange under 8% of the colored area.

COMPOSITION:
Horizontal 3:2 canvas. The blue structure is slightly left of center, with its tallest loop reaching y=20% and lowest curve reaching y=82%. Keep at least 10% white margin on left and right and at least 14% above and below. The structure should occupy about 72% width and 62% height. The print remains graphic and readable at 600px wide. No border, no frame, no card, no surrounding photographed environment. No cropping of the main woven form. A sparse asymmetric composition with a bold visual center.

INK AND PAPER:
Only two printing inks: Carolina blue #4B9CD3 and vermilion-orange #EB613F. White uncoated paper #FAFCFD provides the third value. The deepest places are dense BLUE lines, never black shadows. Ink is entirely flat in value, with no gradients inside shapes. Paper is neutral/cool white, not ivory or beige. Use slight ink starvation in some strokes, very fine uncoated-paper tooth, and microscopic registration imperfections. This texture is restricted to the actual ink edges and a whisper of paper grain. It must not create an evenly distributed noisy filter. Lines have tiny natural deviations, as if originally cut into an engraving master.

LIGHTING / DEPTH:
There is NO depicted illumination, NO shadow, NO reflection, NO physical stage, NO perspective camera. Treat the image as a perfectly frontal scan of a paper print under even neutral light. Any suggestion of volume arises exclusively from the spacing and curvature of flat parallel ink strokes. No photorealistic materials, translucency, glass, shiny metal, rim lighting, ambient occlusion, or 3D-render vocabulary.

AVOID:
Literal neural-network node charts, glass panels, glowing circuits, brains, robots, eyes, hands, random floating shapes, stock tech imagery, photorealistic objects, wireframe CGI, isometric design, gradients, blur, glow, drop shadows, beveled edges, pastel mist, washed-out blue, excessive distressed paper, arrows, labels, letters, numerals, logos or watermarks. The result must be a striking authored mid-century optical screenprint with an unmistakable warm-orange accent.

## Divergent objectives

Use case: stylized-concept.
Produce one original landscape 3:2 editorial artwork for an AI alignment website. Artwork only; no captions, words, numerals, borders, branding or mockup. This image belongs to a matched series of Carolina-blue and vermilion-orange optical screenprints.

SPECIFIC AESTHETIC / ART HISTORICAL DIRECTION:
1960s Swiss concrete art meets the best experimental mathematical book-jacket design, executed as a two-color hand-pulled silkscreen print. The image is absolutely FLAT. Its sophistication comes from composition, line rhythm, optical tension, and physical ink, not from photorealistic rendering. Use the stark certainty of a geometric poster, the irregularity of ink printed onto cool white uncoated paper, and engraved concentric contour patterns. A restrained, visually surprising, intentional piece of graphic art. NOT a CGI sculpture, NOT an isometric scene, NOT a stock scientific illustration.

CONCEPT:
The distinction between the objective we intend and the objective a system actually follows. Depict TWO adjacent gravitational-looking contour fields seen exactly face-on from directly above. They are flat drawn maps, not actual holes or physical terrain. One field, on the left, is a broad, off-center oval made of twenty-four closely nested irregular ellipses in Carolina-blue ink. Its outer shape is about 45% of the frame width and 72% of the frame height; its center is at x=33%, y=48%. The inner white aperture is small but clearly open, near x=34%, y=43%. The ellipses should be subtly asymmetric rather than computer-perfect circles, and the spacing varies with mathematical purpose to suggest an attractive basin through line density alone.

The second field, on the right, is smaller, denser, more sharply pinched, centered around x=72%, y=60%, about 30% of the frame width and 48% of the frame height. It is also a family of nested blue outlines. Where the large and small fields approach one another, the outer contour strokes bend into a narrow saddle, like a deliberately simplified mathematical phase portrait. Each line remains crisp and individually legible; no dense murky tangle. The result feels like a printed scientific topography with a poetic, almost Op Art tension.

THE WARM ACCENT:
Place a single flat vermilion-orange disc approximately 9% of the canvas width directly over the small right-hand field, slightly off its mathematical center. The orange disc is a solid ink shape, not a ball: no highlight, no shadow, no shading, no edge bevel. It overprints the blue contour lines, which can be faintly seen through the pigment where they cross its footprint. From the empty central aperture of the large left-hand field, a single confident orange trajectory line curves upward through the saddle and bends down into this orange disc. A short small gap immediately before reaching the disc gives a printmaker's deliberate rhythm. The curve has no arrowhead. One orange disc and one orange line only; the orange is a focused 8–12% accent and creates the entire narrative of the image. No scattered colored marks.

COMPOSITION:
Landscape, 3:2. Cool white paper remains visible around every side, at least 8–10% margin. The two unequal forms have a lively asymmetrical balance. Large left shape slightly higher, smaller right shape lower. Leave generous white at upper right and lower left. Forms may approach but never touch the image boundaries. The drawing must have impact when reduced to a 550-pixel-wide website image. No horizon, no ground plane, no perspective, no framing device, no distant mountains, no physical substrate under the drawn maps.

COLOR / MATERIAL:
Exactly two saturated pigments, Carolina blue #4B9CD3 and warm vermilion-orange #EB613F, printed on white #FAFCFD. Blue linework must be saturated and matte, neither pastel nor navy. Warm color is orange-red, not red-red, yellow, gold or pink. Allow microscopic misregistration, a few tiny ink pinholes, slight variation in stroke edge pressure, and extremely faint paper tooth. No fake aging, creases, stains, distress texture, yellow cast, beige, or grain filter laid over everything. The image should look freshly printed by an excellent artist-printer.

LIGHTING:
No represented lighting whatsoever. A flat, uniformly lit scan of a flat paper artwork. Any visual depth must arise purely from flat ink line spacing. No cast shadows, ambient occlusion, atmospheric depth, 3D reflections, specular highlights, extrusion, glass, translucent sculpture, wireframe rendering or gradients.

AVOID:
3D topographic models, paper-cut stacks, balls, plinths, machine parts, UI network diagrams, robots, brains, hands, floating cubes, vague painterly mist, sci-fi, gradients, glow, glossy surfaces, decorative random glyphs, lettering or watermarks. Stay uncompromisingly in the language of a two-ink mid-century optical screenprint.
